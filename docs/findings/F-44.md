# F-44 — The inline cap bounds node count, not IR depth; only field runs are flat

- **Assumption**:
  - ADR-0040 "Inline cap": a node count (`INLINE_CAP = 65 536`) and an expansion nesting of 512 keep a compile bounded.
  - F-42 "Change made": #70 lowers path chains with a loop, so a chain of any length compiles and runs.
- **How it fails** (the #70 standards review at de34e3f; both NOT_RUN, the red must observe them):
  - **Inlining multiplies depth.** 300 defs `def fi: [`×100 `f(i-1)` `]`×100, called as `f300`, stay under both caps (about 30 001 nodes and 300 expansions), yet the inlined Collects nest about 30 000 deep. `lower`, `IrVerifier` and `Compiler` recurse on the IR, and F-42 overflowed at about 44k frames on the 512 KiB stack of ADR-0017.
  - **Only field reads are fused.** `Resolver.chain` fuses runs of `.a` into one `FieldRun`. Any other step kind (`.[0]`, `.[]?`, slices) still builds a left-nested Pipe as deep as the chain, and the same recursive passes walk it. jq-ref accepts 21 844 steps of `.[0]` (rc 0, the #70 spec review).
  - **No DEVIATIONS row for long def runs.** jqj compiles any number of top-level defs, but jq-ref rejects 10 000 with `memory exhausted` (DefChainTest javadoc).
- **Severity**: blocking for #70 (an untrusted filter can crash the compiler, the F-42 class).
- **Evidence**: the #70 standards review at de34e3f; Resolver.java `expand`, `chain`; IrVerifier.java:112; Compiler.java:82.
- **Change made** (#70):
  - The resolver tracks the IR depth it builds. Past `Resolver.MAX_IR_DEPTH = 512`, the filter is a Stub, like the 512th nested expansion. A DEVIATIONS row records it, since jq runs such filters; spec 2b's calls revisit it.
  - The number was first ruled 4 096. #70 measured nested Collects through defs on a 512 KiB thread: cold, 625 levels run and 650 overflow; warm, 4 096 run and 8 192 overflow (probe at f7670a7). A cold JVM is the case a host meets, so the bound is 512, the parse depth (#81), with margin under 625.
  - Every step kind of a postfix chain with constant keys and bounds lowers to an IR whose depth does not grow linearly with the chain, with tick counts equal to the nested form. Computed-key steps count toward the IR-depth bound (ruling below).
  - DEVIATIONS gains the long def run row, and the "Long path chains" row names the step kinds its tests cover.
  - ADR-0040 is amended in the same merge.
- **Measured at 4e9f444** (#70, probes in the implementer's scratchpad, 512 KiB thread, fresh JVM per run):
  - Without JaCoCo, a fresh JVM runs 512 levels (8/8) and stubs 513 (6/6). After a stubbed 30 001-level compile, 512 runs only 6/8; 496 runs 8/8.
  - Under JaCoCo (the gate), after a 30 001 Stub, 512 runs 0/8 and 480 runs 8/8. The 513 Stub itself overflowed at the guard's throw, the first string concat at depth.
  - So "512 levels on 512 KiB" holds for a fresh JVM, not in every JVM state. The same is true of #81's parse depth of 512, which this bound equals. Lowering the bound would stub #81's ratcheted depth cases.
- **Ruling on the measurement**: keep 512. The guard does no allocating or class-loading work at depth: it throws a signal with no message or stack trace, and the Stub message is built where the compile unwinds. The 512-runs edge is pinned in a fresh child JVM without an agent, the case a host meets. The Stub edges are pinned in the suite. Making lower, IrVerifier and Compiler non-recursive is spec 2b's work (its trampoline), not #70's.
- **Measured at 025adcf**: with the stackless signal, every agent-free state stubs 513 and 30 001 (4/4 each). Under JaCoCo, a warmed JVM runs out of a 512 KiB stack at about 500 levels during the descent, before the guard. That is the instrumentation's frame cost (about 1 KiB per level), not a host's.
- **Ruling**: every 512 KiB edge (512 runs, 513 and 30 001 Stub) is pinned in fresh agent-free child JVMs. The guard's logic is also pinned in the suite on a 16 MiB thread, so coverage sees the branch. The stack claim is a fresh, uninstrumented JVM's.
- **Found at b0b5095** (both #70 re-reviews): the guard counts one level per Ast that `lower` builds. It misses nesting built elsewhere: the value-parameter Binds `expand` wraps around a body, and the Bind per computed-key step in `Links.bound` (`.[.a]`, `.[.a:]`, `."a\(.b)"`). A chain of those, or 500 nested value-parameter expansions, passes the guard with about 1 000 or more levels. My earlier carry of computed-key steps contradicted "every step kind" above.
- **Ruling**: the bound is on the depth of the built IR, as IrVerifier and Compiler recurse on it, wherever the Resolver builds a node: `lower`, `expand`, `Links` and the balanced pipes. Past 512 the filter is a Stub. Computed-key chains are bounded this way rather than flattened, and the DEVIATIONS row "IR depth" names every construct this stubs, with jq-ref's result: nested defs, value-parameter nesting, computed-key chains (jq-ref runs 2 000 steps of `[.a:]`), and operator chains longer than 512 terms (jq-ref runs 600 terms of `.a+`; #86 lifts it).
- **Not changed here**: Binder and Resolver still recurse on left-associative operator chains (`.a+.a+…`, `and`, `or`), which the parser builds in a loop. That existed before #70 and is ticketed separately.
- **Change made (#86)**: the Binder (`binary`, `operands`, `inLevel`/`rightFirst`), ConstantKey's
  `fold` and the Resolver's `operators()` walk an operator chain's left spine in a loop, keeping
  the compile levels, the operand order, the spends and the fold of a constant prefix. The IR shape
  is unchanged; operator runs past `BIND_RUN` become one Binds in #94 (ADR-0043). The 512 KiB edge's
  dependence on the JIT tier is F-49.
- **Amended by F-54 (spec 2b)**: past the inline cap, past `MAX_IR_DEPTH` or at the 512th nested
  expansion, a call is a PLAIN `Call` to a function lowered once from a worklist, so nested
  definitions and nested value parameters compile and run. The ruling's "past 512 the filter is a
  Stub" now holds only where no call is cut: computed-key chains and alternating operator chains.
