# F-94 — the specialisation census meets filters built to exceed every cap

- **Assumption** (ADR-0045 #117, S5): the census over the corpus shows no refusal under
  `SPECIALISE_CAP`; a refusal raises the cap, up to `INLINE_CAP`, and a refusal above that is a
  finding.
- **How it fails**: three of #114's differential cases are built to exceed `INLINE_CAP`:
  `over-cap/param-600`, `over-cap/param-unused-600` and `over-cap/closure-doubling-20`. Against a toy
  specialiser built to the design they are refused (405, 393 and 6 keys); uncapped, their copies
  would be 51 M, 50 M and 6 M nodes (the #117 test author's numbers, UNVERIFIED on the real pass).
  `refused == 0` over the whole corpus contradicts #114's required cases, so the census as first
  written was a wrong red, not a strict one.
- **Severity**: minor.
- **Evidence**: the #117 census hand-back (t/117-specialise at bc3ddcf); the grilling-adviser's ruling
  of 2026-11-15.
- **Change made**: refused by design, no cap raise. ADR-0045 #117 "Order and cap" names the three
  ids; the census asserts the refused set is exactly that list, each listed filter is also refused
  under `INLINE_CAP`, and S5's number is computed over the other filters only. GLOSSARY "Specialisation
  cap" gains one sentence.
- **Open**: keys are spent in Source order, so a parameter chain can spend the cap before a later
  `recurse` key. On jq-ref, `<param-600's defs>; 0|g600(.) | last(limit(30000; 0|recurse(.+1)))`
  gives `29999`. #117's red gains this case; a refusal of recurse's key there is a finding against
  S2/S5's order. Closed by F-96: the order stays, the filter is named.
