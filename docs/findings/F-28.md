# F-28 — the node lease, its self-fence and in-doubt runs

Date: 2026-11-05 · Task: P2-15a · ARCHITECTURE section: §4 (lifecycle), §7 (WorkQueue, `nodes`) (version 3.4)

- **Assumption in the document:** §7's WorkQueue row lets any worker claim a row once its item lease ran out; nothing tells a
  dead node from a slow one, so a paused node (GC, VM steal, a frozen container) is taken over while it still sends (D-101's
  "false takeover"). D-101 (4) records a `sending` state before every send to tell an in-doubt run.
- **How it fails in practice:** a node paused past its item lease resumes and sends what its successor already sent; and a
  per-send record costs a commit round trip on the measured bottleneck (bench/RESULTS.md CORE-15 preview).
- **Severity:** major
- **Evidence:** NodeLeaseTest, NodeLeaseFencePropertiesTest (jqwik: lost, delayed and late renews, pauses), MembershipContract
  (in memory and PgMembershipIT, a renew racing a release on two connections), PgWorkQueueIT / InMemoryWorkQueueTest (liveness,
  doubts, restart), FlowWorkerFenceTest, PgWorkQueueStatementsIT (no statement per message).
- **Change made:** a node lease beside the item leases. V14 `nodes(node, epoch, lease_until)` (null = released) and
  `work.doubts`. A node joins at start (epoch + 1; its own unheld items wait for the previous incarnation's lease end, `old lease_until` —
  that incarnation's fence closed half its own lease before, and the new incarnation's lease need not be the old one's, so
  nothing is subtracted: at most L/2 more wait per restart) and
  renews every R (`FLOTTILA_NODE_LEASE` PT30S ≤ PT10M, `FLOTTILA_NODE_LEASE_RENEW` PT5S ≤ L/4) on a dedicated connection, one
  statement per renew, at most one in flight; a renew unanswered L/2 after its send fails there (the fence closed by then), its
  answer is ignored, the lease connection is closed and reopened, and the next tick renews again — never unbounded. Another node's item is claimable only when its holder has no live row; a hand-out
  of a row left unsettled counts a doubt (derived in-doubt: nothing written per send). The claim, parking a step's failure and
  parking an item past maxAttempts are fence points too: a fenced worker claims nothing (a fenced poll answers 0, no TAKE) and
  writes no DLQ row (FencedException — carrying the step's failure as its cause for a step's — item left unsettled), so a
  fence closed before the park adds no second DLQ row to its successor's (a fence closing between the check and the DLQ
  write still can — FIX-135). A node releases expired peers only when
  its own fence stayed open for the last L.
- **Timing argument:** let `s` be the node's monotonic reading just before a renew is sent. The database starts the renewed
  lease at its own `now()` ≥ `s`, so `lease_until` ≥ `s + L`. The node's fence closes at `fenceUntil = max(s + L/2)` over the
  renews answered renewed, so its last send starts before `s + L/2`. A peer releases the row only after `lease_until` passed,
  i.e. at least `L/2` after the node's fence — a margin for clock-rate drift and the release's own latency; a renew applied
  after the expiry but before the release keeps the row (the release asks for expired rows only, the row lock serialises
  them). A renew that answers not renewed supersedes the node: fence closed for good, fail-stop (exit 3). A database outage
  stops every renew alike; the "open for the last L" rule keeps the first node back from releasing the others. Takeover ≈ L + R.
- **Assumptions of the timing argument:** (a) the node's monotonic clock runs while the node is stopped: true for a GC pause,
  SIGSTOP or CPU steal, but `System.nanoTime` (CLOCK_MONOTONIC) stops during a VM or host suspend, so a node resumed from one
  finds its fence open for time that passed in the database, and only its next renew (superseded → fail-stop) stops it; a
  send it starts before that renew answers is not covered. (b) The database's wall clock does not step: `lease_until` is
  measured by its `now()`, and a forward step larger than L/2 lets a peer release the row while the node's fence is still
  open — the margin is void. (c) The rejoin margin: a restarted node's own items wait for the old `lease_until`, a point the
  old incarnation's fence passed L_old/2 before on (a)'s clock; a previous process that still runs (no fail-stop yet) is
  covered by the same margin for the items it held at the join; an item it claims after the join (its fence still open, no
  per-epoch record in Postgres) can be reclaimed by the new incarnation when the item lease runs out — safe only while the
  item lease is at least L/2.

- **For the owner's review:** derived in-doubt (D-112, draft) over-reports a dead node's in-flight items and items left
  unsettled by a fenced or leaving run, whether or not they were sent; since P2-15b (D-113) a send
  is bounded by the fence (see "Sends bounded by the fence" below); the fence is read before a
  claim's TAKE and before a park's DLQ write, so a fence closing in between counts a doubt or adds a second DLQ row (FIX-135); `WorkQueue.Item` equality ignores
  `doubts` (existing suites compare items unchanged; accepted for now, architect 2026-11-06).
- **Sends bounded by the fence (P2-15b, D-113 draft, amends D-100):** a worker's send gets a `Deadline` that is the earlier of
  the message's deadline and the fence's `openNanos` (core `node.Fence`, implemented by `NodeLease`), re-read at each
  `remaining()`; a producer starts nothing with no time left and cuts a send still open when a re-read finds none (http resets
  the request: no reply stored once the cut fires while the reply is still streaming; a reply already received whole may still
  be stored (race)). The builder arms a timer at `openNanos` just before calling the producer: a fence renewed meanwhile re-arms
  it, a closed one ends the run's wait with `FencedException` — not retried, not parked, the item unsettled and its rerun in
  doubt — while the abandoned send keeps its target permit until its own future settles; a producer failure landing while the
  fence is closed is a `FencedException` carrying it. **Supersession** closes the fence between two timer readings (openNanos
  drops to 0 at once): the timer does not see it; the node's fail-stop (exit 3) ends every send at once instead; until the exit
  an answer that lands is taken, a failure is fenced. Not covered still: (a)'s VM suspend (the monotonic clock stopped, the
  fence reads open), and the time between the check and the producer's first byte (the same check-then-act as a claim).
