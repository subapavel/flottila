# F-112 — a bench filter built to sit one key past SPECIALISE_CAP fails #117's census

- **Assumption**: ADR-0045 "Order and cap": the census asserts that the filters refused under
  `SPECIALISE_CAP` are exactly the refused-by-design list, and a refusal moves the cap to the next
  power of two.
- **How it fails**: #119's `bench/cap-specialise-over.1k` is one key past the cap on purpose (demand
  16 390), so Table B can show a refused specialisation beside its pair `bench/cap-specialise.1k`.
  `SpecialiseCensusTest` reads it as an unexpected refusal and goes red. Moving the cap never
  converges: the cell is re-measured to sit at the new cap.
- **Severity**: minor; it blocks #119's gate only.
- **Evidence**: `.logs/119-gate.log` in the impl/119 worktree; grilling-adviser, 2026-11-16.
- **Change made**: owner, 2026-11-16 (Fable's recommendation A): ADR-0045 gains a second list, "at
  the cap by design", holding `bench/cap-specialise-over.1k`. The census asserts that each entry is
  refused under `SPECIALISE_CAP`, refuses nothing under `INLINE_CAP`, and that its pair refuses
  nothing; S5 is computed over filters in neither list. The test author edits `SpecialiseCensusTest`
  on this ruling; #119's Touches gain that line. Excluding the bench family was rejected as a
  weakening of the oracle.
