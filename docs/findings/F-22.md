# F-22 — a live JFR stream loses the thread of virtual-thread events, so CPU per step reads completed recordings

Date: 2026-11-02 · Task: FIX-108 · ARCHITECTURE section: §4 threading (:153), §8 three screens (version 3.4)

- **Assumption in the document:** D-094 / FIX-108 packet (Shape): one ordered `RecordingStream` matches every `jdk.CPUTimeSample`
  to the innermost `io.flottila.Step` event on the same virtual thread, both delivered with their `eventThread`.
- **How it fails in practice:** on JDK 25.0.5 a virtual thread's thread constant goes to a per-carrier buffer that a flush writes
  only after a leased checkpoint (a platform thread starting or ending, a type-set change); a flush writes the events before the
  type set, and a live stream resolves each flush as it reads it — so `getThread()` is null when no leased checkpoint fell in the
  window (jdk25u jfrTypeManager.cpp:123, jfrCheckpointManager.cpp:637-660, jfrRecorderService.cpp:640, cited by the implementer).
  The sampler threw NPEs (bench: 1 534 step + 29 sample traces, executions 19 827 of 21 000) and, once null-safe, under-read CPU
  (CpuSamplerIT 2 of 10 runs: 302–518 null-thread samples of ~1 600, estimate/expected 0.67–0.76; bench: 3–4 % of step events,
  1.5–2 % of samples).
- **Severity:** major (the documented mechanism is replaced; the intent — CPU per flow and step within a stated error — is kept).
- **Evidence:** agent/work/FIX-108-probe2/Probe2.java and its logs (not committed): live stream nulls in 2 of 10 runs; the same
  chunk dumped and read whole resolves every thread, 0 nulls in 20 of 20; a platform thread started every 100 ms prevents it (0 of 10).
- **Change made:** the sampler keeps a plain on-disk `jdk.jfr.Recording` and, every read cycle (5 s, scheduled through the injected
  `Timer`, read on the sampler's virtual thread), dumps the recording, reads it whole with `RecordingFile`, feeds only the window
  between the cuts of the two previous cycles (JFR's own times of both cuts, taken from the same dump as the events — a dump whose
  first chunk differs shifts every time by 28–53 µs — exactly once), drops the chunks written before the previous cycle's dump (a
  maxAge lost windows when CPU-bound bursts delayed the reader by up to 2.4 s), and only then commits its own cut (cut 0 at start),
  so a cut always follows its dump. A dump lacking the opening cut is a WARNING, retention exceeded: the window opens at the last fed
  end (±53 µs). One lacking the closing cut too feeds nothing; the next window reaches back. The aggregate trails the present by one to two cycles. Null-thread events stay counted (steps: execution; samples:
  unattributed) as a defence. Public API, JSON, event and env key unchanged.
- **ARCHITECTURE.md edit:** §4 :153 sentence and the 2026-11-02 changelog row name the read cycle and the lag; version stays 3.4.
- **PROGRESS entry:** FIX-108 marked `Deviation: F-22`
- **For the owner's review:** the dump costs a chunk rotation and a copy of two cycles (the window read and the one being written, at most 64 MiB) of JFR data every 5 s (bench in
  bench/RESULTS.md); rejected: the event carrying its thread id (fixes steps, not samples), a platform thread per flush (HotSpot
  internals, the executor rule), widening the IT bound by the null share (a weakened test), re-resolving nulls against a later dump.
