# F-59 — a contract suite waits in real time for an event; only a failing case pays the patience

Date: 2026-11-16 · Task: FIX-240 · ARCHITECTURE section: §11 (version 3.33)

- **Assumption in the document:** `CLAUDE.md`, Tests: "no sleep-based timing — a fake clock drives
  backoff, leases and deadlines". §11's table gives the component contract suite "seconds". Read
  together, no test of `rail8-testkit` waits on wall-clock time, and FIX-240's row called the
  10 s of its failing run "a wait on wall-clock time, which CLAUDE.md forbids".
- **How it fails in practice:** the suites' `soon(condition)` (`ComponentContract.java`:109,
  `PollingConsumerContract.java`:93) polls `System.nanoTime()` until the condition holds or
  `patience()` is spent (10 s; :93, :88). No fake clock can drive it. What it waits for is an
  event on a thread of the component under test - the handler was called, a file is gone - and
  the suite is one text for the mock and for the real component on a real socket and a real
  server, where no injected clock moves the other side. It is a bounded wait for an event, not a
  timing assertion: it returns the instant the condition holds, and no result depends on how long
  that took. The component's own timers - the poll interval, backoff, leases - are the fake
  clock's in every suite (`tick()`; the self-test's `advanceToNextRound`).
  So a passing case spends no patience, and a case that fails because its event never comes
  spends one: it stops at its first `soon` that times out. `PollingConsumerContractSelfTest`
  runs the polling suite against deliberately broken variants and has to see those failures, so it pays:
  its `patience()` is 2 s (:323). Measured test by test in one run of the module (40.93 s for the
  class): seven of its 22 tests take 40.25 s and the other fifteen 0.66 s together. The seven are
  whole multiples of the patience - 12.05 s, 10.03 s, 4.05 s and three of 2.03 s, sixteen waits
  of 2 s in all - and 8.04 s for the variant whose two failing cases each join for twice the
  patience. Two of the sixteen depend on the run, by a reading of the contract's lines (a case
  that fails with or without a wait, as its threads fall); across twenty runs the class took
  37.8 to 42.0 s, in steps of about 2 s (44 to 47 s on four busy cores). The removal variant's
  test is five waits: 10.03 to 10.07 s (10.2 s under load), before and after FIX-240. That is
  the "10.09 s" of FIX-240's row
  and the "39.6 s" of the row the merge dropped:
  the price of observing failures, bounded by the patience times the cases that fail by a wait
  (twice the patience where the wait is a join),
  paid by testkit's test of itself. No component's suite pays it while it is green.
  The flake was something else. The broken variant removed its file on the round's thread after
  it had started the handler's thread, and the case read the directory once it saw the handler's
  call: two unordered acts of the test's own variant. FIX-240 orders them.
- **Severity:** minor — the rule was read wider than it is written; no mechanism changes.
- **Evidence:** `agent/work/FIX-240-20runs.log` (twenty runs of the class, all green; class time
  37.8 to 42.0 s, 44.3 to 46.6 s under load; the removal test 10.05 to 10.07 s) and
  `agent/work/FIX-240-before.log` (40.03 s and 10.06 s before the change);
  `agent/work/FIX-240-evidence.txt` (the time of each of the 22 tests in the module's verify
  run, from its surefire report; which line each wait is at is read from the contract by the
  test-author and the reviewer, not measured); `soon` and the `patience()` overrides read by
  the scout (`agent/work/FIX-280-scout.md`, Part 1).
- **Change made:** none in the contract suites: `soon` stays a real-time bounded wait for an
  event. The self-test keeps its 2 s. A shorter patience would cut the class's time in
  proportion; whether a healthy case's event still arrives inside it on a loaded machine was
  not measured, and a healthy case that times out would flip an expected set the other way - a
  flake of the kind this row removed. What `soon` is not for: a timer of the code under test.
  Found on the way and rowed: the contract's reads of "nothing was removed" are one instant
  (F-60, CONSIDER-30); two leftovers in the self-test's other variants (FIX-281).
- **ARCHITECTURE.md edit:** none (minor). `CLAUDE.md`'s sentence stands as written: `soon` is no
  sleep and drives no backoff, lease or deadline.
