# Packet FIX-229 (class B) — a recorded send has not yet taken its hold

Rows: `agent/BACKLOG.md` FIX-229 (and the first flake of FIX-38, the same shape), under MAIN-1. Seen on
a runner, `ci` run 213, the Postgres 17.11 job (`agent/work/MAIN-1-ci.md`):

`DeploymentsRedeployTest.everyStartWaitsForEveryStop:558 c's held message: not within PT10S`

## The reading (the scout's, unverified — the red confirms it or you hand back)

`platform/testkit/src/main/java/dev/rail8/testkit/RecordingComponent.java`, `send`: :207 records the
send (`sent.add`), :209 counts down the `awaitSent` waiters, and only then :210 takes its hold
(`holds.pollFirst()` — a FIFO of latches, taken by the next send of ANY producer, not keyed by flow or
envelope). `awaitSent(n)` (:111-131) returns at :209. A test that does `hold(); send a; awaitSent(3);
hold(); send c; awaitSent(4)` (`DeploymentsRedeployTest` :542-547; `DeploymentsTest` :869-874) can
therefore run its second `hold()` and second send between a's :209 and :210: c's send polls first and
takes a's latch, a takes c's. Releasing "c's" latch then releases a, and c's answer never comes — the
message above. The scout found no product path that drops the answer (`Delivery.java` :175-183 completes
it before any stop-side wait; the stop's timers need a clock advance the case never gives).

## Acceptance

1. The cause is shown by a deterministic red in the testkit's own unit tests, at the component's level:
   two sends and two holds in the order above, with the first send stopped between being counted for
   `awaitSent` and taking its hold — and the first latch then releases the wrong send. The window needs
   a seam: a package-private hook of `RecordingComponent` in the manner of the testkit streams'
   `beforeDrainExit(Runnable)` (FIX-12) — name it and state where it runs (on the sending thread, after
   the send is counted and visible, before `send` returns) at the top of the test class; the test must
   stay meaningful after the fix (in the fixed order the hold is already taken when the hook runs). If
   you find a way to force the order through the public surface alone, prefer it and say so. If the
   forced order does NOT release the wrong send, stop and hand back what you saw.
2. The property the fix must give, pinned by the tests: once a send is visible — to `awaitSent`, to
   `sent()` and its like — it has taken its hold; holds are taken in the order the sends are recorded
   (two concurrent sends cannot record in one order and take holds in the other). `holdNext(n)` with
   n > 1, a send with no hold armed, and a hold armed after a send was recorded (it waits for the next
   send, never reaches back) keep their behaviour — one case each if none exists.
3. The fix is the implementer's, in `RecordingComponent` only (record and take as one step, the hook
   where the tests say): no sleep, no new lock held across a caller's code or a blocking wait; the
   class's public surface does not change. No test of another module is edited; `DeploymentsRedeployTest`
   and `DeploymentsTest` are not touched.
4. Survey in the test-author's hand-back: the other testkit doubles that record and then arm or take
   something in a second step observable through an `await…` (`PollingConsumer` doubles, the mock
   component, `HoldingMessageStore`-like fixtures of core and runtime) — each with one sentence: the
   same window, or why not. Fix none; the architect opens rows.

Order of work: test-author (the red tests; the hook's signature assumed, so the red may be a compile
