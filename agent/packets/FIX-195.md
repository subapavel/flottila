## The policy to re-record as D-146

When the window `timer.schedule` throws — `RuntimeException` or `Error` — the batches the drain
just took are **committed, never failed**. They stay in flight, reach the sink in the order taken,
and whatever stayed queued rides their completions. Nothing stays queued with no commit in flight
and no window, and no item is failed by the refusal.

What it replaces: one refusal failing up to `maxBacklog` rows plus every waiter, never retried.
What is sacrificed: tempo, and the newest rows under sustained load. That trade is one-sided for
trail rows, which is why the owner took it.

Observability: a counter that always rises, and a WARNING on **the first refusal of an episode and
on recovery** — not once per refusal. Two lines per episode, so a permanently refusing timer
cannot flood the log and silence stays impossible.

The rule to pin: **no public method of `BatchingWriter` ever completes by throwing; every throw
becomes the item's failure cause.** Not swallowed — it is logged with its cause.

## The shape — check it before following it

The session branch's designer proposed: `drainLocked` loses its `while(true)` (one take, one arm
attempt, return); `Refused`, `Drained.refused` and the refused half of dispatch go away; the
result becomes `Drained<T>(List<List<Pending<T>>> commit, Throwable windowRefusal)`, with the
refusal logged and counted in dispatch.

That shape was designed against the pre-rework class. **Verify it against the class as it now
stands before adopting it.** If the rework changed the drain, the dispatch or the waiter path
enough that this shape no longer fits, say so in the hand-back with the file and line, propose what
does fit, and stop — do not improvise a different design silently.
