The stage: both connections of the relayed pool (size 2) are made busy on a query held at the relay; the Hold step is then
released; the body waits, bounded, until the store's flush is pending. Before the stage a flush completes (asserted). The
running item's step-1 snapshot write and its settle start only after the Hold step is released — in the old teardown that
release came after the relay was closed. The statement that threw is named by elimination (the reported stack has async
frames only): the stop and the release wait for nothing, the opened handle writes through the direct pool, and only the
store's close waits on the relay; its flush joins the pending write and rethrows its failure.
It is the fixture's fault: the case's claim — both ids answered, both rows leased, exactly one entry — is asserted before the
pending write, and the product does what it should with the refusals (a WARNING; the body stays durable without a row; the
settle's failure is logged and the item left to its lease, FlowWorker :450-451). Why it was rare: in the green runs the late
write lands after the writer was closed and is dropped with "writer closed", which nobody waits for.

The fix (PgIntakeHandoffGroupIT only; the Log subclass untouched; nothing under src/main): the teardown first drains —
releases every frame the case held, stops the worker, releases the Hold step (for m-1 and m-2 also ahead of their entry: a
slot may enter after the body ended), waits bounded for the worker's close and for the store's flush and asserts both — and
only then closes the relay, the world, the pool and the database. Every step runs; the first failure is thrown with the
later ones suppressed on it; a wait not met fails with what it waited for. The staged order stays at the end of the case.
