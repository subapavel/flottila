# F-108 — HugeConstantKeyTest's 10 s compile limit has a 1.6× margin

- **Assumption**: `HugeConstantKeyTest.anArrayLiteralFoldsInLinearTime`
  (jq-core HugeConstantKeyTest.java:88) asserts that the compile of a 10⁶-element literal plus `.`
  ends within 10 s, as a check that the fold is linear.
- **How it fails**: alone, the test takes 6.13 s and 5.95 s (load average about 2.1 on 4 vCPU;
  `.logs/118-hugekey.log` in the #118 worktree). Under a second Maven build it timed out at 10 s twice:
  in #118's gate (7e2f327, `.logs/118-gate-final.log`) and earlier on impl/117. The rerun gate passed.
  So the limit tests the machine's load as much as linearity.
- **Severity**: minor. The gate stops at jq-core and skips later modules, which costs a rerun.
- **Change made**: none; the test is unchanged. A follow-up ticket should pin linearity by a ratio
  instead (the time at 10⁶ against the time at 10⁵, within a bound), or by a step or allocation count,
  and keep only a loose absolute limit. The owner decides whether the follow-up enters spec 2b.
